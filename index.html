Five agents I built and run for music work. Each one reads the sources a person would open by hand, writes what it finds into a sheet or a page, and does the boring part on a schedule, every day or every few days, without being asked.

Nothing here is a chatbot. They are scrapers, matchers and builders with strict rules about what they may touch. Each section below runs a small working version in your browser.

A&R Superplaylist

Daily · Spotify → Sheet → Site

Scans 44 discovery playlists for everything added in the last seven days. A song is only interesting if two or more lists picked it up in the same week, so that overlap is the whole test. What survives gets filtered down to small artists, logged with its streaming and follower numbers, and tracked week over week so growth is visible.

The dashboard it publishes is encrypted and password-protected. It is a mirror of the sheet, so the team reads one set of numbers.

Runs: every morning. Writes new rows to the music tab only, never to flags or notes. Builds a private playlist of the week's finds three times a week.

    Show list and announcements

    Daily · NYC / CHI / LA

    Pulls the next two weeks of concerts in three cities, plus everything newly announced, from public listings, 19 venue calendars and the official Ticketmaster API. It clears duplicates across sources, matches artist names that are spelled three different ways, and marks the rows the team cares about.

    One page comes out of it, password-protected, with a change report showing what moved since the last run.

    Runs: a venue sweep most days, a full three-city scan on Mondays, and a review pass weekly. Reads only public pages and the official API.

      Itinerary builder

      On demand · Sheet out

      Five answers in: artist, how many shows, the room size to start in and the room size to finish in. It lays out the dates, routes the cities so nobody drives backwards, and climbs the room ladder evenly from the first night to the last, then writes the whole thing into the label's sheet format.

      A fuller version audits a routing that already exists: finds the gaps, fixes a ladder that jumps too fast, and researches what comparable artists played in each city.

      Runs: when asked. The real one picks named rooms from a venue bank; the demo shows the ladder and the routing only.

      AXS resale scan

      Every 3 days · Sheet in and out

      Walks every upcoming show in the tracker and re-reads three prices: the standard ticket, the official resale, and the open market. When the cheapest way in is resale rather than face value, the row gets flagged, because that says something about how the on-sale went.

      Fees change the answer, so the comparison can be run before or after them. It reads prices only. It never signs in, never holds a ticket and never buys.

      Runs: every three days. Updates each tab in place and reports what changed.

      Label map

      Reference · One file

      A single-page answer to who owns whom across the majors: the three parents, the labels under each, and the distribution arms that confuse everyone. Built from a company directory plus manual research, with the logos inlined so the file works offline and forever.

      The PDF poster is generated by parsing the HTML, so the page and the print version can never drift apart.

      Runs: rebuilt by hand when ownership changes. No network calls, no dependencies.
